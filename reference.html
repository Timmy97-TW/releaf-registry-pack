What each part page should contain

The shape of a registry part page, the rule for each kind of documentation entry, how a figure is embedded — and then all 33 parts, each shown as it should end up.

How to use this. The console tells you what to do next. This page tells you what finished looks like. When the two disagree, this page is the intention and the console is the mechanics.

The anatomy of a part page

A registry part page has three parts. Only the third is something you write repeatedly.

1
Functional description The field at the very top, edited from the part's own edit screen. One paragraph: what the part is, what it does, and every sub-part linked by id. This is the only text most readers will read, so it carries the claim.
2
Construct The ordered list of sub-parts. The registry assembles this itself from what you registered — there is nothing to paste, but if the order is wrong the sequence is wrong, so it is worth checking once.
3
Documentation entries Separate entries, created from the Documentation panel, each with a type and a title, each written in Markdown, each shown to the reader as its own collapsible block. A part may have several. This is where almost all the work is.

The five types, and what belongs in each

The registry offers five. The rules say to treat them as guidance rather than a template, but being consistent across 33 parts is worth more than being clever on any one of them, so these are the lines we are drawing.

TypeWhat goes in itWhat does not
designWhat the sequence is and where it came from. The rationale. The sub-parts and their roles. Any modelling that informed the design. The literature.Results. If a number came out of an experiment, it belongs below.

The ELISA question, settled. ELISA goes in characterisation. It measures how much protein the part makes, which is behaviour, not construction. There is a second reason: Best Basic Part and Best Composite Part are judged largely on characterisation, and quantitative data filed under Experiment does not read as characterisation to someone scanning the page.

How a figure is embedded

There is one pattern, and every part already on the registry follows it. The image goes on its own line with an empty alt text, and the legend is a separate paragraph underneath, beginning with a bold Figure N. and its title. The registry re-encodes whatever you upload to AVIF and stores it under that documentation entry's own id, so the URL appears after you upload, not before.

What a legend has to say

A legend is read on its own, by someone who has not read the page. So it carries, in this order: what the panel is, the method in one clause (strain, construct, n, conditions), what to read off it, and the scale — ladder, markers, error bars. It claims nothing the panel does not show. If the image is not the team's own, it is attributed.

Never AI-generated. Evidentiary figures are the team's own data. This applies to every image in this pack.

Overview

This is the assembled Level 2 construct of our stress-responsive optogenetic bioreactor. It carries four Level 1 transcriptional units on a single plasmid, so that Bacillus subtilis can sense and respond to light signals on its own. The first module makes the chromophore PCB, the second expresses the sensor kinase CcaSm3, and the third expresses the response regulator CcaR. The fourth is the output, which secretes ACC deaminase (AcdS) when green light is present - specifically, CcasR-Csn-AcdS-Histag (Bba_2654HAF3), the wildtype output with the Csn signal peptide.

Literature

moves to the protectant collection

How the circuit works: CcaS binds PCB through its GAF domain and is made in a green-light sensitive ground state. Absorbing green light switches it to an active state that autophosphorylates and passes the phosphate to CcaR. Phosphorylated CcaR activates the PcpcG2 output promoter. Red light returns CcaS to the ground state and probably causes CcaS to dephosphorylate CcaR, which turns output off (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019; Hirose et al., 2008). Because B. subtilis does not make PCB, the circuit needs all four modules on the same plasmid to work.

Replaced by: Pveg versus PcpcG2-veg switch logic is in the **Light-sensing circuit** collection, *Design*.

Why optogenetic: Our original plan was to release engineered rhizobacteria that sensed stress themselves, which limits the usable stressors, and crops also differ in their thresholds. For example, heat stress in Arabidopsis starts at about 30–37 °C, whereas peanut tolerates about 35–40 °C. Using light as the only input lets the user program thresholds per crop without redesigning the circuit.

Differences from the published system: Castillo-Hair et al. (2019) drove ccaS from the xylose-inducible PxylA and ccaR from the IPTG-inducible Phy-spank. We replaced both with constitutive promoters (PliaG and Pveg) so that light is the only input and no inducer dosing is needed.

Output module: PcpcG2-veg is promoter from Castillo-Hair et al. (2019), in which the sequence after the −10 hexamer comes from the strong Pveg promoter while the CcaR binding site stays intact. In the original study this gave much higher output than PcpcG2-172. The EEBD (E. coli expression-blocking device) sits upstream of the RBS to stop leaky expression of the output gene in E. coli during cloning, before the construct is moved into B. subtilis. The output gene is a fusion of a Sec signal peptide, P. putida acdS, and a 6xHis tag. In the SamyQ version, 12 of the first 15 acdS codons were recoded to open up the mRNA structure around the start codon.

Use in plant protection

Replaced by: The circuit-level discussion is in the **Light-sensing circuit** collection, *Discussion*.

The aim is to release plant-protective proteins only when and where they are needed. A secreted ACC deaminase lowers stress ethylene around roots, so it could be used as part of a plant-growth-promoting bioprotectant. Light as the only input offers a practical advantage over circuits that sense stress themselves. Crops differ in their stress thresholds (for example, heat stress starts at about 30 to 37 °C in Arabidopsis but about 35 to 40 °C in peanut), and a self-sensing strain would have to be redesigned for each stressor and each crop. With light control, a user can set thresholds for each crop through a microcontroller-based sensor that switches the light, without redesigning the genetic circuit. This is the role the circuit plays in our bioreactor system.

A modular platform for other protectants

Modules 1 to 3 form a reusable light-sensing chassis, and only Module 4 defines the output. Any of our protectant cassettes can in principle be placed behind PcpcG2-veg:

  • AcdS lowers ethylene levels. This is the version built and tested in the full circuit.
  • LEA14 protects cellular structures under environmental stress. We did not build the light-responsive Module 4 with LEA14. Instead, we characterized the constitutive LEA14 version to see how it would behave in the whole system, and it also protects plants facing salinity stress. This avoids repeating the same assembly, since ccaSR-AcdS already demonstrates that the concept works.
  • BoPep4, a broccoli plant elicitor peptide, activates plant defense responses.

Our constitutive constructs, which use Pveg in place of the light switch, give the maximal secretion and production of each protectant. They also serve as a benchmark for the light-controlled version.

Beyond our project

Replacing the xylose-inducible and IPTG-inducible promoters of the published CcaSR system (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019) with constitutive ones means no inducer dosing is required. Other teams can reuse the four-module Level 2 plasmid for any secreted protein, simply by exchanging the output cassette. This makes light-controlled secretion in B. subtilis usable beyond plant protection, for example for other enzymes or peptides that should be produced on demand.

Use in plant protection

Replaced by: The circuit-level discussion is in the **Light-sensing circuit** collection, *Discussion*.

The aim is to release plant-protective proteins only when and where they are needed. A secreted ACC deaminase lowers stress ethylene around roots, so it could be used as part of a plant-growth-promoting bioprotectant. Light as the only input offers a practical advantage over circuits that sense stress themselves. Crops differ in their stress thresholds (for example, heat stress starts at about 30 to 37 °C in Arabidopsis but about 35 to 40 °C in peanut), and a self-sensing strain would have to be redesigned for each stressor and each crop. With light control, a user can set thresholds for each crop through a microcontroller-based sensor that switches the light, without redesigning the genetic circuit. This is the role the circuit plays in our bioreactor system.

A modular platform for other protectants

Modules 1 to 3 form a reusable light-sensing chassis, and only Module 4 defines the output. Any of our protectant cassettes can in principle be placed behind PcpcG2-veg:

  • AcdS lowers ethylene levels. This is the version built and tested in the full circuit.
  • LEA14 protects cellular structures under environmental stress. We did not build the light-responsive Module 4 with LEA14. Instead, we characterized the constitutive LEA14 version to see how it would behave in the whole system, and it also protects plants facing salinity stress. This avoids repeating the same assembly, since ccaSR-AcdS already demonstrates that the concept works.
  • BoPep4, a broccoli plant elicitor peptide, activates plant defense responses.

Our constitutive constructs, which use Pveg in place of the light switch, give the maximal secretion and production of each protectant. They also serve as a benchmark for the light-controlled version.

Beyond our project

Replacing the xylose-inducible and IPTG-inducible promoters of the published CcaSR system (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019) with constitutive ones means no inducer dosing is required. Other teams can reuse the four-module Level 2 plasmid for any secreted protein, simply by exchanging the output cassette. This makes light-controlled secretion in B. subtilis usable beyond plant protection, for example for other enzymes or peptides that should be produced on demand.

Literature

Light sensing: Holo-CcaS is produced in a green light-sensitive ground state with low autokinase activity, with a photostate absorbance maximum at 535 nm. Absorption of a green photon switches CcaS to a red light-sensitive active state with high autokinase activity, with a photostate absorbance maximum at 672 nm. The active kinase transfers a phosphoryl group from ATP to a conserved histidine, and from there to a conserved aspartate on CcaR, which activates CcaR and induces transcription from the PcpcG2 output promoter. Red light reverts the active state to the ground state and de-activates PcpcG2 transcription, probably through CcaS-mediated dephosphorylation of CcaR (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019). The 535 nm and 672 nm are absorbance maxima of the CcaS photostates (Hirose et al., 2008), and are distinct from the illumination used in the published characterization, which was delivered by a Light Plate Apparatus with 520 nm green and 660 nm red LEDs (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019).

Dependence on the PCB module: CcaS is photoactive only as a holoprotein with PCB bound to its GAF domain, and B. subtilis does not make PCB natively. This module therefore has no function on its own. It depends on the PCB supply module PtagC+RBS-Ho1-pcyA-L3S2P21, which converts endogenous heme to biliverdin IXα and then to PCB inside the host. In the Level 2 system the two modules are carried together for this reason.

Choice of the miniaturised variant: We used ccaS-m3 in place of full-length ccaS. The miniaturised variants of Nakajima et al. (2016) remove the two PAS domains, whose function in CcaS is not known, and several of them regulate gene expression better than the wild-type kinase. Castillo-Hair et al. (2019) tested ccaSm3 and ccaSm10 in B. subtilis PY79 and reported that both increased dynamic range about ten-fold, to 71 ± 12-fold for ccaSm3 and 62 ± 12-fold for ccaSm10; the two were not statistically different in B. subtilis (p \= 0.49) and ccaSm10 gave lower overall output, so the system containing ccaSm3 was named B. subtilis CcaSR v1.0. The same PAS deletions applied in the E. coli CcaSR v2.0 context gave a v3.0 sensor four times less leaky, with a dynamic range of nearly 600-fold and no deleterious effect on green light sensitivity or response dynamics (Ong & Tabor, 2018). We took ccaS-m3 as the better-supported choice on both counts, low background and high dynamic range.

Promoter strength for the sensor: The published system expresses ccaS from the xylose-inducible PxylA promoter and ccaR from the IPTG-inducible Phy-spank promoter (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019), so the levels of both proteins are set by chemical inducers (Haller et al., 2025). Our bioreactor is intended to run on light alone, without xylose or IPTG dosing, so we replaced the inducible promoter with a constitutive one. This substitution is our own design decision and is not part of the published system. We chose a weak constitutive promoter deliberately. Castillo-Hair et al. (2019) found that output falls again at high CcaS levels, probably because PCB becomes limiting and the resulting apo-CcaS has phosphatase activity. A weak promoter keeps CcaS inside the range where the PCB supplied by the PCB module can be loaded onto it. PliaG is one of the weaker σA promoters in the Bacillus BioBrick Box set, whose constitutive promoters span more than three orders of magnitude (Radeck et al., 2013).&#x20;

Literature

Signal transduction: CcaS and CcaR form a two-component system. Under green light the active CcaS kinase transfers a phosphoryl group from ATP to a conserved histidine in its own kinase domain, and from there to a conserved aspartate on CcaR. Phosphorylation activates CcaR, which then induces transcription from the PcpcG2 output promoter. Red light reverts CcaS to its ground state and de-activates PcpcG2 transcription, probably through CcaS-mediated dephosphorylation of CcaR (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019). In the original description of the pathway, autophosphorylation of the CcaS histidine kinase domain is up-regulated by green light, and CcaS then phosphorylates CcaR, which induces cpcG2 expression (Hirose et al., 2008). CcaR carries the light signal from the sensor to the output promoter, and the light-responsive expression module cannot respond to light without it.

Why a constitutive promoter: The published system drives ccaR from the IPTG-inducible Phy-spank promoter (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019). Replacing it with constitutive Pveg is our own decision, made so that light is the only input to the circuit and the bioreactor needs no inducer dosing. Pveg gives high, continuous expression, and Castillo-Hair et al. (2019) report that of all the cyanobacterial genes in their study ccaR was the only one that did not initially suffer from poor expression. Expressing CcaR constitutively also means its level no longer tracks an inducer, so it becomes a fixed property of the strain.

Background activation of CcaR: This design's main risk is on the CcaR side. In the published v0.1 system, adding IPTG alone, which raised CcaR without any green light, increased sfGFP output up to 40-fold, probably because CcaR can be phosphorylated by a small-molecule donor such as acetyl phosphate (Castillo-Hair et al., 2019). The amount of CcaR in the cell therefore sets how much output the circuit gives in the dark, and a constitutive promoter fixes that background. Dark-state and red-light output must therefore be measured for this module in our strain and reported alongside the green-light output; a fold-change quoted without a measured background would not be meaningful. These measurements have not yet been made. If the background proves too high, the correction is a weaker promoter or a weaker RBS on this module.

Dry lab / computational analysis

moves to the protectant collection

The mRNA secondary structure around the ribosome binding site and start codon heavily influences the translation-initiation efficiency inside bacteria. Synonymous codons, meaning codons that result in the same amino acids, in the 5’ end of a CDS can impact how the mRNA folds near the binding site. The folding can either hide or expose the Shine Dalgarno sequence and start codon, impacting the efficiency of ribosome binding and translation initiation. Following this concept suggested by Castillo-Hair (2019), the design pipeline treated the first 15 codons of the LEA14 CDS as an editable region. ViennaRNA 2.7.2 was used to generate and fold tens of thousands of synonymous variants in the RS-inclusive 236-nt 5’ window (residual recombination site + MF001 RBS + spacer + SamyQ ORF + first 90 nt of cds), determining the mean base-pair unpaired probability across the 15 nt following the ATG and across the MF001 Shine-Dalgarno core (AAGGAGG) and creating a balance score.

Replaced by: The 5′ recoding pipeline is described in the **Protectant modules** collection, *Design*; only this variant’s codon changes and scores stay here.

SamyQ-LEA 2.1-Histag was determined to be the best candidate from the run: 6 of the first 15 LEA14 codons were changed (c1 GCG→GCA, c2 TCT→AGT, c3 TTA→CTC, c4 TTA→CTC, c10 TTT→TTC, c11 GTA→GTC). The predicted start+15 accessibility improved from 0.538 to 0.669 and SD-core accessibility decreased from 0.357 to 0.307 when compared to the wild type, resulting in an overall balance score gain of 0.895 to 0.976. The codon adaptation index against B. subtilis 168 usage dropped from 0.85 to 0.81. Instead of following Castillo’s rule, which prioritizes low GC content and A over U, the program individually folded and verified every candidate to verify improved accessibility, as the Castillo-rule-applied control produced a start+15 score of only 0.669 and an SD-core score of 0.078 (balance 0.748), which was lower than wild type.

The mRNA secondary structure around the ribosome binding site and start codon influences translation-initiation efficiency inside bacteria, independent of the encoded protein sequence. Following the concept suggested by Castillo-Hair (2019), the design pipeline treated the first 15 codons of the BoPep4 CDS as an editable region. Dry lab / computational analysis

moves to the protectant collection

The mRNA secondary structure around the ribosome binding site and start codon heavily influences the translation-initiation efficiency inside bacteria. Synonymous codons, meaning codons that result in the same amino acids, in the 5' end of a CDS can impact how the mRNA folds near the binding site. The folding can either hide or expose the Shine Dalgarno sequence and start codon, impacting the efficiency of ribosome binding and translation initiation. Following this concept suggested by Castillo-Hair (2019), the design pipeline treated the first 15 codons of the acdS CDS as an editable region. ViennaRNA 2.7.2 was used to generate and fold tens of thousands of synonymous variants in the RS-inclusive 236-nt 5' window (residual recombination site + MF001 RBS + spacer + SamyQ ORF + first 90 nt of cds), determining the mean base-pair unpaired probability across the 15 nt following the ATG and across the MF001 Shine-Dalgarno core (AAGGAGG) and creating a balance score.

Replaced by: The 5′ recoding pipeline is described in the **Protectant modules** collection, *Design*; only this variant’s codon changes and scores stay here.

SamyQ-AcdS 2.1-Histag was determined to be the best candidate from the run: 12 of the first 15 acdS codons were changed (c2 TTA→TTG, c3 AAT→AAC, c4 AGA→CGC, c7 AGA→CGC, c8 TAT→TAC, c9 CCG→CCC, c10 TTA→CTT, c11 ACA→ACT, c12 TTC→TTT, c13 GGA→GGG, c14 CCG→CCA, c15 AGC→AGT). The predicted start+15 accessibility improved from 0.563 to 0.773 and SD-core accessibility improved from 0.088 to 0.203 when compared to the wild type, resulting in an overall balance score gain of 0.651 to 0.976. The codon adaptation index against B. subtilis 168 usage dropped from 0.85 to 0.75. Instead of following Castillo's rule, which prioritizes low GC content and A over U, the program individually folded and verified every candidate to verify improved accessibility, as the Castillo-rule-applied control produced a start+15 score of only 0.698 and an SD-core score of 0.186 (balance 0.884), which, although still an improvement over wild type, remained lower than the balance achieved through individual verification of every candidate.

Overview

This part encodes residues 15 to 23 of BoPep4, a 23-residue plant elicitor peptide from broccoli (Brassica oleracea var. italica), with its native start and stop codons removed so that it can be fused to a signal peptide and a tag.

BoPep4 acts as a peptide hormone: it binds the plant PEPR receptor and raises defence signalling and salt tolerance. This construct keeps only the conserved C-terminal core, the part of the peptide that makes contact with the receptor, and removes the N-terminal residues entirely.

Why this truncation

The full-length peptide and a ladder of N-terminal truncations were compared in molecular dynamics, scoring how reliably the Asn23 clamp with the receptor is held across a trajectory. The ladder is not monotonic. Full length, 7–23 and 15–23 all hold the clamp above 98% of frames, while the intermediate 9–23 construct falls to 77.1% clamp integrity and 46.9% bidentate occupancy. Shortening the peptide is therefore not what costs the contact; the loss at 9–23 comes from removing the Lys7 anchor while leaving a flexible N-terminal tail behind, and cutting further back to 15–23 removes that tail and restores the clamp.

This part is the short end of that ladder, carried as a candidate for the smallest construct that still engages the receptor.

Overview

This part places the Escherichia coli expression blocking device (EEBD) in front of the ribosome binding site RBS0 (BBa_26OSLRE4), so that one Level 0 part provides both cloning protection and translation initiation.

The EEBD is a B0015 double terminator (BBa_B0015) set between two recombination sites (BBa_K1679038). While a construct is being assembled and propagated in E. coli, the terminator stops transcription from reaching the coding sequence, which prevents unwanted expression and the accumulation of a product that would be toxic to the cloning host. The device is removed on transfer to Bacillus subtilis, leaving RBS0 to initiate translation.

Every constitutive and light-responsive protectant cassette in this collection carries this part in the same position, between the promoter and the protectant coding sequence.
